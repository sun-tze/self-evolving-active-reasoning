Examples and error analysis
- Case 29: A/B identify a fire-resistant suit, matching the target's key mechanism.
  A scores 0.6622; the shorter B scores 0.4839. C/D propose a safe room / hidden compartment.
  The first history also denies relevant safe-room claims. A higher lexical score does
  not ensure faithfulness, and a lower lexical score does not necessarily mean a worse mechanism.
- Case 70: D improves over B by 0.3694 F1 but still proposes a hidden passage; the target
  mechanism is pausing a VR simulation to obtain coffee. This gain cannot be described
  as solving the puzzle more correctly.
- Case 7: B scores 0.9288 but proposes a deception by friends and family. The target mechanism
  is a hospital record mix-up causing a mistaken death declaration: high F1 with semantic error.
These comparisons use offline targets only after generation. Targets were never added
to model requests. No cases were replaced in response to outcomes.

Research interpretation at the time of the pilot
Mean F1 did not improve with the evidence writer; the first-selected dialogue marginal
effect was also close to zero. The positive interaction is a hypothesis for later testing;
five exploratory cases cannot establish generalization.
The next research step should add offline semantic correctness and dialogue faithfulness
criteria and then freeze a follow-up design, instead of optimizing lexical overlap alone.
If these five cases are used for development, reserve the remaining 65 for confirmation.
No further GPU run was performed as part of this pilot report.
